Assume a smooth divergence-free solution of unforced 3D incompressible Navier--Stokes on $\mathbb{T}^3$ exists on $[0,T)$. Let $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ and $M_\varepsilon(t)=\|\omega*\eta_\varepsilon\|_{L^\infty}$.

\paragraph{Auxiliary field.}
\[
\partial_t\phi+u\cdot\nabla\phi=|\omega|^2,\qquad \phi(0,x)=0.
\]
Along characteristics, $\|\phi(t)\|_{L^\infty}\le\int_0^t M(s)^2\,ds$. This upper bound is linear in $t$ whenever $M$ is bounded below by a positive constant. It is not a uniform constant on $[0,\infty)$.

\paragraph{Pointwise stretching.}
At an interior maximum of $|\omega_\varepsilon|^2$ the viscous pairing is non-positive, and Calder\'on--Zygmund gives a bound of the schematic form
\[
|\omega_\varepsilon\cdot((\omega_\varepsilon\cdot\nabla)u)|\le C_{\mathrm{CZ}}(3)\,M_\varepsilon(t)^2,
\]
up to the standard logarithmic correction in the Beale--Kato--Majda estimate. The pure quadratic bound without a logarithm is the classical CZ estimate on $\nabla u$ in BMO, not a closed $L^\infty$ bound.

\paragraph{Lyapunov inequality, as stated.}
\[
\frac{dS_\varepsilon}{dt}\le C_{\mathrm{abs}}\bigl(1+M_\varepsilon(t)^3\|\phi_\varepsilon\|_{L^\infty}^{3/2}\bigr)-\kappa'\int_{\mathbb{T}^3}|\omega_\varepsilon|^6\,d\lambda.
\]
\textbf{Gap A.} Substituting the history bound makes the prefactor grow like $t^{3/2}$ if $M_\varepsilon$ remains positive. The comparison equation is not the autonomous ODE $y'=Cy^2-\kappa''y^3$.

\textbf{Gap B.} The relation $\int|\omega_\varepsilon|^6\ge c M_\varepsilon^4$ does not follow from H\"older. H\"older supplies $\|\omega_\varepsilon\|_6\le M_\varepsilon$ on a unit-volume torus. A lower bound requires a lower bound on the measure of $\{|\omega_\varepsilon|\gtrsim M_\varepsilon\}$.

\paragraph{What would close (B).}
A proof must produce $\int_0^T M(t)\,dt<\infty$ for every finite $T$, for every smooth divergence-free finite-energy $u^\circ$, with $f\equiv 0$, and then invoke Beale--Kato--Majda plus parabolic regularity. Neither the tether degeneracy identity $B(F,H)\equiv 0$ nor a holographic analogy supplies that integral bound.
